Ages 4–5

A full sensory immersion in the natural world.

Every day, young children move between garden, kitchen, art space, and music circle — planting, cooking, painting, singing, and crafting in Spanish and English.

Inspired by Waldorf rhythm, Reggio curiosity, Forest School wildness, and the living traditions of Costa Rica — this group gives young children a day that is nourishing from first song to last story. No desks. No screens. Just hands, soil, color, sound, and wonder.

Children paint with watercolors and natural pigments. They model with beeswax and clay. They make pottery. They learn songs on flute, drum, and voice. They bake bread, tend seedlings, go on nature walks, and learn the names of plants that feed, heal, and color. They hear stories told by teachers who know that imagination is the foundation of all future thinking. They move their bodies through creative movement, music, and the joyful beginnings of capoeira, acrobatics and circus play.

Mornings follow a gentle, nourishing Waldorf-inspired rhythm — the gift of security through repetition. At this age there's no nap: after lunch, children move into bilingual classes and Reggio-inspired project exploration — following their curiosity, building, creating, and making things that matter to them. Pick-up is at 2 pm.

Dunnia leads our youngest learners, ages 1 to 5. Jenne leads permaculture gardening, nutrition, and movement.

For older children ready to go deeper. The same philosophy — but with more complexity, more skill, more challenge, and more responsibility.

Permaculture design and biodynamic gardening. Pottery. Sewing and weaving with natural fibers and plant-based dyes. Nature crafts with found materials. Herbalism and plant wisdom — from seed to medicine to dye to story. Ensemble music — singing, drumming, flute, and world instruments. Acrobatics, circus arts, sports, and capoeira. Bread baking and healthy nutrition rooted in real food culture. Natural pigments, watercolor, and beeswax. Earth and ocean stewardship woven into every week.

Inspired by Waldorf's understanding that arts are curriculum, Reggio's trust in children's own questions, Summerhill's belief in real agency, and Montessori's cosmic frame — this group gives children a day where every activity connects to every other. The plant they grow becomes the food they cook becomes the dye they use becomes the story they tell.

This group follows Cosmic Education in English, connecting biology, history, geography, numbers, and letters within the great story of life on Earth. Alongside personalized and rigorous academic learning, children explore patterns in nature, origin stories, archetypes, and Indigenous ways of knowing through storytelling, observation, imagination, and hands-on discovery. They learn to read the world as they develop the skills to read, write, reason, and work confidently with numbers.

Afternoons are dedicated to project-based learning — children choose, plan, and build real projects over weeks, developing focus, collaboration, and the deep satisfaction of finishing something that matters.

Full day

Ages 10–13

8 am – 2 pmNovember 23, 2026 to March 19, 2027 (Thanksgiving to Easter)Solstice to Equinox (June to September 2027)

For the ones who are ready to lead.

This program takes everything the younger programs plant and grows it into something bigger — real skills, real responsibility, and real contribution to the community and the living world. Guided by the full range of Wild Child's pedagogical inspirations — and grounded in the democratic education tradition of Summerhill and the indigenous wisdom of Costa Rica — this program trusts young people with meaningful work and meaningful questions.

  • The land: Advanced permaculture and biodynamic gardening. Regenerative design. Food sovereignty — understanding where food comes from, who controls it, and how communities can feed themselves. Fermentation, preservation, and traditional food preparation. Cooking real meals for the community.
  • The arts: Deeper work in natural pigments, pottery, carpentry, sculpture, weaving, and sewing. Nature crafts as cultural practice. Music as ensemble and as performance.
  • The body: Acrobatics, circus, and capoeira — with increasing mastery, discipline, and collaborative choreography. The body as instrument of strength, grace, and expression.
  • The mind: Cosmic Education in English—weaving biology, ecology, history, and geography with Indigenous ways of knowing and the exploration of archetypes. Reading, writing, research, and critical thinking unfold through meaningful projects and real questions rooted in the natural world.
  • The community: Leadership and ecological activism — not as theory but as practice. Children in this group take on real stewardship roles within the Wild Child community. They mentor younger children. They design and lead projects. They speak up for the land and the ocean. They begin to understand that they are not just growing up in Costa Rica — they are responsible for it.

Afternoons are entirely project-based — long-arc, community-facing projects that leave something behind when the season ends.

Learning the names of the trees. Understanding why the rivers matter. Hearing the stories told here for generations.

Spanish immersion happens the way language is truly learned — through song, story, play, and daily life.

Not taught. Absorbed.

Healthy nutrition is part of the curriculum at every level. Children who grow food, cook food, and understand where food comes from develop a relationship with nourishment that lasts a lifetime.

Wild Child Nosara

Worldschooling Hub

Wild Child is more than a school.
It is a landing place for globally minded families seeking community, continuity, beauty, and deeper roots.

Over the years, Wild Child has naturally become a home for worldschoolers, unschoolers, homeschooling families, creatives, entrepreneurs, artists, remote workers, and nomadic families moving between cultures, countries, and ways of life.

Many of the families who find us are raising children beyond the boundaries of conventional education. They believe learning happens everywhere through travel, relationship, nature, culture, creativity, meaningful work, and lived experience.

We do too.

At the same time, we understand that nomadic families also need grounding. Rhythm. Belonging. Trusted adults. Consistency. Community.

Wild Child offers a place where children can feel rooted while remaining open to the world. A place where families can arrive for a few weeks, a season, or longer, and immediately feel part of an ecosystem of relationships. Some families come during the Peak Season and return every year. Others stay through the Green Season for deeper immersion. Some integrate homeschooling or online academics alongside our programs. Others seek personalized pathways blending Waldorf, Montessori, Reggio Emilia, Forest School, leadership education, movement arts, ocean stewardship, and bilingual learning.

There is no single model of learning here.

Only a shared belief: that childhood should remain alive, relational, meaningful, creative, and deeply connected to the real world.

For many families, Wild Child becomes more than a stop along the journey.

What begins as a vacation in Nosara often turns into a longer stay because families discover a community, rhythm, and way of learning that feels like home.

Our Philosophy

Wild Child doesn't belong to a single pedagogy. Our curriculum is inspired by the wisdom of many traditions — each one chosen because it serves children, this land, and this moment in history.

  • Waldorf gives us rhythm, beauty, and the understanding that imagination is the foundation of all future thinking. That arts are not extras. That a child who kneads bread, tends a seedling, or hears a story told by a living voice is doing the most important work of their life.
  • Reggio Emilia gives us the belief that children are capable, curious, and full of their own ideas. That the environment is the third teacher. That documentation and deep listening matter. That projects emerge from children's genuine questions — not from a predetermined plan.
  • Forest School gives us the outdoors as the primary classroom. Mud, rain, roots, and insects. The understanding that risk, challenge, and unstructured time in nature build resilience, confidence, and a relationship with the living world that no indoor curriculum can replicate.
  • Leadership education and Earth & Ocean Stewardship give children the understanding that their voice matters, that they are not powerless observers of the world but active participants in it. Children are invited to think critically and creatively, to act with alignment and discernment, and to understand sovereignty not as domination, but as responsibility. We believe children are gifts to this world — capable of becoming compassionate leaders, changemakers, bridge-builders, protectors of life, and human beings who know how to stand in right relationship with themselves, others, and the Earth.
  • Summerhill and democratic education give us respect for the child as a whole person with real rights, real choices, and real agency. Children here are not managed. They are trusted.
  • Indigenous wisdom — and specifically the living knowledge traditions of Costa Rica — give us the deepest roots of all. The names of the plants. The ways of reading the land, the water, and the sky. The understanding that humans are not separate from nature, managing it from the outside — we are part of it, accountable to it, nourished by it.
  • Montessori's Cosmic Education gives the older children the biggest possible frame — the story of the universe, the emergence of life, the arc of human civilization — so that everything they learn has a place in a larger whole.

All of it held together by one belief: childhood is not preparation for life. It is life itself.
