THANKSGIVING WEEK AT WILD CHILD NOSARA November 23–27 | 8 AM–2 PM Join us for a special week of ocean exploration, mindfulness, scientific discovery, creativity, and connection with nature! This week marks the beginning of our immersive learning journey for children ages 6–9 and 10–13, rooted in our Four Schools: Wonder, Presence, Performance & Stewardship. SPECIAL OCEAN CONNECTION DAYS November 24–26 Three extraordinary days of ocean education with Dr. Anand Patel, marine biologist and oceanographer, alongside our Wild Child educators. Feeling the ocean's energy & listening deeply to the sea. On the water — Exploring waves, currents, and the movement of the ocean. In the water — Snorkeling, ocean awareness, and connecting with marine life. In the lab — Hands-on scientific experiments, discoveries, and investigations. Mindfulness & Ocean Connection — Exploring breath, presence, deep listening, and our relationship with the sea through mindful movement, observation, and sensory awareness. Throughout the week, children will explore the relationship between science, mindfulness, nature, creativity, and our responsibility to care for the Earth. Ages: 6–9 and 10–13 Full immersion week: November 23–27 Hours: 8 AM–2 PM Ocean Connection Days: November 24–26 Small groups: Maximum 8 children per age group for the special ocean experience. A week to awaken wonder, cultivate presence, deepen our connection with the ocean, and inspire the next generation of Earth stewards. Limited spaces available! Join us for this special week or begin your longer Wild Child immersion. Register: info@dandelionwildschooling.org Wild Child Nosara 🌀

Subject: Wild Child Nosara: Immersion for Ages 6–13: All the Details Dear families, On the summer solstice of 2026, Wild Child Nosara turned four years old. In these four years, we've welcomed more than 500 children from Costa Rica and around the world. Now we're entering a new chapter: a long-term program for ages 1–5, new immersion programs for ages 6–9 and 10–13 built for greater continuity, belonging, and academic depth, and an invitation for parents to join as co-learners, with new activities just for parents and new activities for parents and children together. Below, you'll find complete details on the new immersion programs. We hope you'll consider joining us, so the magic can keep deepening. A Small, Intimate Learning Community Wild Child offers small learning groups where children receive close mentoring, personalized academic guidance, and the time and attention needed to develop their individual interests and voices. Our approach is project-based, relationship-based, and hands-on. Children develop both savoir-faire (the knowledge and skills to make and do) and savoir-être (the capacity to be present, know themselves, exercise discernment, relate well, and participate meaningfully in community). Alongside these, we nurture the many forms of intelligence each child carries, so that every child has the chance to recognize and grow their own particular gifts. Rigorous academics are woven into real questions, practical experiences, creative work, and sustained interdisciplinary projects. We hold this integration through four interconnected dimensions of the learning experience: the School of Wonder, the School of Presence, the School of Performance and the School of Stewardship. These are not separate classes. They are interwoven throughout each day. Our Daily Rhythm 8:00–9:00 | Leadership and the School of Stewardship The day begins with Jenny and guest teachers through biodynamic gardening, permaculture, survival skills, regenerative design, healthy nourishment, capoeira, games, sports, movement, and meaningful responsibilities. Leadership and stewardship sit at the heart of Wild Child. Carina guides children into real responsibility for the Earth and ocean through concrete projects, community participation, and regular field journeys to beaches, forests, farms, libraries, and local environmental and cultural initiatives. Once a week, children meet at the beach at 7:00 am for capoeira with Julian, an essential capoeira teacher in the Nosara community. Parents are warmly invited to join the class. 9:00–11:45 | Academics and the School of Wonder Our two lead teachers alternate, offering complementary pathways into learning. Karen leads Cosmic Education, connecting biology, ecology, history, geography, mathematics, reading, writing, and research with Indigenous and local ways of knowing, creation stories, mythology, identity, and archetypes. Benjamin, our lead educator, conceived and created the Schools of Wonder, Presence, and Performance. An artist, musician, writer, filmmaker, and educator, Benjamin blends personalized academics with music, poetry, philosophy, creative writing, critical and imaginative thinking, visual art, storytelling, and performance. The 6–9 group develops strong foundations in reading, writing, and mathematics through storytelling, imagination, nature observation, concrete materials, and artistic expression. The 10–13 group moves more deeply into independent research, complex mathematics, sustained writing, philosophy, cultural history, ethics, critical thinking, and interdisciplinary projects. 11:45–12:00 | Storytelling Hearth Children gather for storytelling in English or Spanish: creation stories, mythology, poetry, literature, local histories, archetypal journeys, and stories created and told by the children themselves. 12:00–12:30 | Shared Lunch A wholesome organic meal and an opportunity to practice gratitude, conversation, community, and a joyful relationship with nourishment. 12:30–2:00 | Projects, Ateliers and Craftsmanship Dunia holds the afternoon rhythm primarily in Spanish. Subject: Rewilding Childhood — Wild Child Nosara Dear Wild Child families & friends, As the end of peak season approaches, we begin to notice the first signs of seasonal change in Nosara. The air remains warm and dry, while the trees are beginning to grow new leaves, a quiet indication that the rainy season is on its way. At Wild Child, we pay close attention to these transitions. They guide our rhythm, our presence, and the way we learn together. At Wild Child, we are reimagining education. Wild Child is a heart-centered learning environment, more like a village than a school, where children feel at home, held by rhythm, nature, and relationships. We are open year-round, and families can start and pause anytime, in alignment with worldschooling, unschooling, and life in movement. Our Approach At the core of our curriculum are two essential principles: attunement to the world we live in, and right relationship with oneself, others, and all living beings. Nature is our primary teacher. Curiosity is our curriculum. We draw from Waldorf, Forest School, Summerhill, and Reggio Emilia inspirations, while co-creating an emergent curriculum with rhythm as our heartbeat: Dandelion Wildschooling, rooted, adaptable, and responsive. Rewilding Rewilding is at the heart of our intention. For us, it means: • Returning to a felt connection with nature • Reclaiming the body as a source of intelligence • Trusting curiosity, play, and intrinsic motivation • Restoring rhythms that honor life rather than rush it • Living in reciprocity with the Earth and each other Rewilding is the process of coming back into right relationship with life. For us, education is not preparation for life. It is life. We don't block movement, travel, or curiosity; we support it. We listen deeply. At Wild Child, we trust life and we love life. For families who need it, we can offer guidance with documentation and certification. If you are visiting Nosara, curious, or seeking a nurturing, reliable, and beautiful space for your child, we would love to connect. Florencia Figueroa

Artist, sculptor & art teacher

Florencia is an Argentine artist and art teacher who has made her home in Costa Rica. She holds a degree in Fine Arts with an emphasis in Sculpture from the Universidad de Costa Rica, where she graduated with distinction, and first trained as a drawing teacher at the Escuela Nacional de Bellas Artes Manuel Belgrano in Buenos Aires.

She has taught in the Sculpture Department of the Universidad de Costa Rica and as an art teacher at Saint Mary School in Escazú and Tree of Life International School in Santa Ana. Here in Nosara, she teaches art at Casa de las Estrellas and Playhouse.

Florencia directed the commissioning of fourteen sculptures for the Museo del Jade de Costa Rica, assisted with restoration at the university’s School of Visual Arts, and for twelve years co-ran Onírica Art Shop in La Fortuna. Alongside her studio practice she has trained in transpersonal art therapy, art therapy, and creativity coaching, bringing a gentle, process-centered approach to working with clay, wood, wax and metal—from the first idea and sketch to the finished piece.
